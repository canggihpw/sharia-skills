# Source hierarchy and bibliography

Every recommendation in this pack must trace to a named source. This file is the
shared bibliography and the citation rulebook. Individual skills list the subset
they rely on and link back here.

## Citation rules

1. **Prefer primary sources**, in this order:
   regulator → fatwa body → standard-setter → exchange/index provider → issuer
   filing → peer-reviewed or university-press scholarship → reputable financial
   press.
2. **Record four things** for every operative fact: value, `as_of`, source, and
   status (announced, in force, phased, or proposed).
3. **Cite the edition and date** for standards and methodologies. A threshold
   without an edition is not a basis.
4. **Separate the layers**: religious texts and fiqh establish the principle;
   standards and regulators set the operative rule; textbooks and journals
   explain method. Do not present a heuristic as if it were a fatwa.
5. **Never fabricate** a page number, decision number, URL, or edition. If the
   exact reference cannot be verified, name the work and say what is unverified.
6. **Do not cite** Wikipedia, blogs, AI summaries, or unattributed aggregator
   sites as the basis for a verdict.
7. **Date every web fact.** Re-verify anything in `thresholds.yaml` marked
   `confidence: medium` or `verify_before_use: true`.

## Where heuristics live

Some outputs use planning defaults — budgeting percentages, allocation tiers,
rebalancing bands. These are **heuristics**, not rulings. Their basis is the
portfolio and budgeting method literature above plus the household's actual
obligations; they are labelled as such and are always user-overridable. Do not
attribute a heuristic percentage to a scholar, a standard, or a regulator.
